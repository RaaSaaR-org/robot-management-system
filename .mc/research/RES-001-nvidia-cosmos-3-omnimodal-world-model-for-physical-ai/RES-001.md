# NVIDIA Cosmos 3 — Omnimodal World Model for Physical AI

> Research entity for evaluating NVIDIA Cosmos 3 for NeoDEM. Findings below are from a
> multi-source, adversarially-verified deep-research pass (25 claims, all unanimous 3-0,
> primary NVIDIA sources). Date of synthesis: **2026-06-28**. Cosmos 3 launched only
> ~4 weeks prior, so deployment surfaces are still moving — re-verify before committing.

## Research Goal

Understand what Cosmos 3 is, how we can access/run it **today** (NVIDIA, Hugging Face,
cloud GPU), and how it could fit NeoDEM — for **testing** and potentially **production** —
given our shift to **cognitive humanoids, specialized on the Unitree G1**.

## TL;DR

- **What it is:** NVIDIA's first **fully open omnimodal "world foundation model"** for Physical AI (launched ~2026-05-31). One **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** model with two towers — an **autoregressive Reasoner** (a Qwen3-VL-initialized VLM that *understands* text/image/video/audio) and a **diffusion Generator** (that *produces* physics-aware video/image/audio/**action**). The two towers have separate params per layer but interact via **joint attention**.
- **One model replaces four.** It unifies what earlier Cosmos split across separate families: **Predict** (world generation), **Transfer** (controlled generation), **Reason** (scene understanding), **Policy** (action generation).
- **Sizes:** **Nano = 16B** (8B reasoner + 8B generator, runs on a single workstation **RTX PRO 6000**); **Super = 64B** (32B+32B, datacenter Hopper/Blackwell for large-scale synthetic data); plus **Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID = 16B** (a ready VLA-style policy fine-tuned on the DROID dataset).
- **Robotics modes:** **Forward dynamics** (action+image+text → future video = *learned simulator*), **Inverse dynamics** (text+video → action), **Policy** (image+text → video+action). Emits **numerical actions** (joint angles, gripper positions, trajectories).
- **Access today:** build.nvidia.com hosts it but is **preview-gated** (our key 404s until enabled in-browser — §4.1); open **BF16 weights on Hugging Face** (`nvidia/Cosmos3-Nano`, `nvidia/Cosmos3-Super`) under **OpenMDW-1.1** (commercial use OK, "Built on NVIDIA Cosmos" attribution); code at **github.com/nvidia/cosmos**; deploy via **NIM** (Reasoner NIM GA, **Generator NIM shipped v1.0.0**) or self-serve via **vLLM-Omni**. Confirmed *working* paid paths today: **DeepInfra** (Nano $0.0108/s) / **Baseten**, or **rented GPU** (Vast.ai RTX PRO 6000 $1.07/hr).
- **There IS a free path — HF Spaces on ZeroGPU (§4.7):** the official **`nvidia/Cosmos3-Action-Viewer`** (forward/inverse-dynamics + policy, on NVIDIA's own L40S) and generator demos (`multimodalart/Cosmos3-Nano`, `akhaliq/Cosmos3-Nano`) are **free to use in-browser**. **HF PRO ($9/mo)** lets you duplicate them onto your own ZeroGPU (40 GPU-min/day, then $1/10 min) — enough to **finish TASK-174 and prototype 175/176**, but **not** to train (177) or run a full-scale dataset.
- **Fit for NeoDEM:** strongest near-term value is **offline** — (a) **synthetic data** to augment LeRobot datasets, and (b) **world-model evaluation**. A **served Cosmos3 policy** is architecturally feasible behind our VLA Server but needs a GPU.
- **⚠️ The G1 gap:** documented embodiments are Franka / UR / WidowX / Google robot / Agibot / AV / egocentric — **Unitree G1 / bipedal humanoids are NOT listed.** Fine-tuning to a G1 is *officially supported in principle* (new embodiment/camera/task) but **undocumented and unverified**; 57D "egocentric motion" is the closest existing action space.

## 1. What it is (architecture, family, capabilities)

### Architecture — Mixture-of-Transformers, two towers
A single MoT model splits the input sequence into an **autoregressive subsequence** (reasoning/understanding via next-token prediction) and a **diffusion subsequence** (generation via iterative denoising). The **Reasoner tower** (AR, initialized from **Qwen3-VL**) interprets multimodal observations and physical context; the **Generator tower** (diffusion) produces physics-aware outputs conditioned on the reasoner. Separate parameter sets per layer, coupled via **joint attention**.
> Caveat: NVIDIA's "single unified forward pass" is marketing-flavored — generation is iterative diffusion across two separate-parameter towers; unification is architectural, not literally one pass.

### Omnimodal I/O
Jointly processes & generates **language, image, video, audio, action**. Generator inputs: text, image, video (±audio), action-trajectory. Outputs: image, video, audio, action, text.
> Caveat: audio is only accepted **embedded in video** (~0.5 s, 48 kHz stereo), not as a standalone input.

### Model family

| Model | Params | Notes | Runs on |
|-------|--------|-------|---------|
| **Cosmos3-Nano** | 16B (8B reasoner + 8B gen) | Efficient inference; dense 8B adapting Qwen3-VL 8B; **BF16 only** (FP4/FP8/FP16 not officially supported) | Workstation **RTX PRO 6000** |
| **Cosmos3-Super** | 64B (32B + 32B) | Max quality; large-scale synthetic data generation | Datacenter **Hopper / Blackwell** |
| **Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID** | 16B | Fine-tuned robot-control (VLA) variant, post-trained on **DROID** | GPU |

### Lineage / how it differs
Earlier Cosmos required orchestrating **four** model families; Cosmos 3 collapses them into one (subsuming VLMs, video generators, world simulators, and world-action models). For reference, the earlier **Cosmos Policy** was a fine-tune of **Cosmos-Predict2-2B** (latent video diffusion, Wan2.1 VAE tokenizer) into a visuomotor policy; and **GR00T N1.7** uses a **Cosmos-Reason2-2B (Qwen3-VL)** backbone — i.e. NVIDIA's humanoid VLA line already leans on the Cosmos-Reason family.

## 3. Relevance to NeoDEM (humanoid / Unitree G1)

NVIDIA positions Cosmos 3 for exactly our space: **synthetic data generation** ("action-conditioned robot data … diverse task trajectories at scale") and **embodiment specialization** (fine-tune to a specific embodiment / camera / workspace / task via open post-training scripts). Three concrete fits:

1. **(a) Synthetic data → LeRobot augmentation** — strongest near-term, offline. Feeds `training-worker` VLA fine-tunes. → **TASK-175**
2. **(b) Learned world-model simulator** for sim-RL policy eval, complementing the MuJoCo digital-twin loop. → **TASK-176** *(low priority — see caveat)*
3. **(c) Served VLA policy** (`Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID`) behind our VLA Server, and/or fine-tuned to G1. → **TASK-177**

**Supported embodiments / action spaces (input shape `(T, D)`):** camera 9D, AV 9D, **egocentric 57D**, Franka+Robotiq 10D (dual 20D), Agibot 29D, UR 10D, Google robot 10D, WidowX 250 10D, UMI 9D.

> ⚠️ **Unitree G1 / bipedal humanoid is NOT a documented embodiment.** Fine-tuning to G1 is *plausible* (new-embodiment fine-tuning is officially supported; 57D egocentric is closest) but **unverified and undocumented** — this is the central open question for our use case.
> ⚠️ **World-model-as-simulator reliability:** NVIDIA itself notes current world models "fall short as reliable simulators of the physical world" — temper expectations for use (b).

### 4.6 Go / no-go for downstream tasks
- **[[TASK-175]] synthetic data → GO** *(task DONE 2026-07-11)*. **Prototype free** on HF PRO via the official Action-Viewer forward-dynamics endpoint (§4.7); **scale** on DeepInfra (Nano $0.0108/s → **1,000 × 5 s 480p clips ≈ $54**) or a rented RTX PRO 6000. - **[[TASK-176]] world-model sim → DONE: NO-GO (now).** Ran the study on HF PRO — 16 forward-dynamics rollouts (n=4 bridge sequences × real + 3 corrupted policies). Confirms NVIDIA's own caveat. ### 4.7 Free path — Hugging Face Spaces on ZeroGPU (verified 2026-06-28)
The build.nvidia.com hosted endpoint is gated (§4.1), but Cosmos 3 **is** reachable for free via HF Spaces.

>
> ⛔ **WHERE THE FREE PATH BLOCKS — fully traced live (2026-06-28).** The action-conditioned endpoint `nvidia/Cosmos3-Action-Viewer-Prerelease` `/generate` requests a **300s GPU job**. Two gates, hit in order:

> 2. **Free signed-in token (read scope):** clears gate 1, the job runs server-side — but then `AppError: exceeded your free ZeroGPU quota (300s requested vs. 299s left)`. **A single forward-dynamics job (300s) exceeds the entire free daily quota (~300s/day).**
>
> **Conclusion: action-conditioned generation (TASK-175/176) requires HF PRO** ($9/mo → 40 min/day ZeroGPU ≈ ~8 forward-dynamics runs/day). HF's own error points to `huggingface.co/subscribe/pro`. The free tier covers only light **text→video** (TASK-174). **Request schema (reverse-engineered):** `/generate` body = `{"dataset","baked_action":[[…action_dim…]],"sample_index","model_mode":"forward_dynamics","prompt_description","num_steps","guidance","seed"}`; it loads conditioning frames from the Space's packaged dataset (`bridge` = BridgeData/WidowX, also `robomind_franka[_dual]`) and needs a real `baked_action` of the dataset's action width — the in-browser viewer supplies this automatically.
>
> ✅ **TASK-175 SOLVED on HF PRO (2026-06-28) — full offline pipeline proven.** `server/curation/cosmos3_synth.py` (`generate`→`convert`) produces a valid LeRobot v2.1 synthetic dataset from action-conditioned forward-dynamics rollouts. Findings that updated the above:
> - **GPU job is ~10–35s, not 300s.** The Space now caps `ACTION_VIZ_GENERATE_GPU_DURATION` at **200s**, and warm-checkpoint runs measured **~10–35s/rollout** (640×480, ~17 frames). So PRO's 40 GPU-min/day is **dozens of rollouts/day**, not ~8. (The earlier "300s>quota" block was real on the *free* tier; PRO clears it.)
> - **Use the raw gradio HTTP API, not `gradio_client`.** `gradio_client.predict` auto-downloads file outputs and **403s** trying to fetch a checkpoint path (`/data/models/nvidia--Cosmos3-Nano`) embedded in the result JSON. Calling `POST /gradio_api/call/generate` + reading the SSE stream, then fetching the returned `/gradio_api/file=…` URLs with the bearer token, works cleanly.
> - **bridge action is 10-D, not 7-D.** The model-space normalizer (`quantile_rot`) expects `[trans(3) + 6D-rotation + gripper] = 10`; the LeRobot `action` column is 7-D euler. Converter does `euler→6D`. (Per-dataset dims/methods in the Space's `normalization_stats.json`: bridge/droid/fractal/umi/robomind = 10, av = 9.)
> - **Validation = real worker code.** `server/src/services/__tests__/synthetic-dataset-validation.test.ts` runs the actual `DatasetService.validateStructure` + `computeQualityScore` over fixture `fixtures/cosmos-synthetic-bridge/` → `valid=true`, no errors. ✅
> - **Still gated:** the real-vs-synthetic fine-tune **ablation** (AC #3) needs a rented GPU + `training-worker` (ZeroGPU can't train). Scale-out to a full dataset → DeepInfra / rented GPU (§4.2–4.3).

### 4.8 TASK-176 findings — world-model-as-simulator feasibility (2026-06-29, HF PRO)

**Verdict: NO-GO (now).** Cosmos 3 forward dynamics should not back the sim-RL `sim_evaluator` yet. Artifacts: `task176-wm-eval/` (`REPORT.md`, `report.json`, `strip_si0.jpg`, `strip_si23.jpg`).

**Method** (`server/curation/cosmos3_wm_eval.py`): for **4 recorded bridge/WidowX sequences**, roll out the *same conditioning frame* under 4 "policies" — the **real** recorded actions + 3 deliberately-corrupted ones (**scrambled / reversed / zero**) — via the HF PRO Action-Viewer (Cosmos3-**Nano**, 480p, 30 steps; **16 rollouts**, ~10–35s each). Predicted clips auto-align to ground truth by matching the conditioning frame (`predicted[0]` → nearest GT frame), so no Space-internal windowing is assumed. Scores: predicted-vs-GT **global SSIM/PSNR**, **foreground-masked SSIM** (where GT moves), and **motion_corr** (correlation of predicted vs real change-maps — a do-nothing policy → ~0, so it is the fair ranking metric).

**Results** — "is the **real** policy ranked #1 (closest to the true future)?"

| metric | real ranked #1 | note |
|---|---|---|
| global SSIM | **0 / 4** | do-nothing `zero` wins all 4 — pixel similarity rewards *no motion* |
| foreground-masked SSIM | 1 / 4 | |
| motion_corr | 1 / 4 | real ranks *last* in 2 of 4 |

The true action sequence's predicted future is reliably closest to reality in only **1 of 4** cases (≈ chance), and the most obvious metric (full-frame SSIM/PSNR) is *actively misleading* — it always prefers a policy that does nothing.

**What works / what doesn't:**
- ✅ **Action-conditioned & visually plausible.** `zero` → near-static (interframe motion ~0.5 vs ~2–5 for moving policies); strips show genuine WidowX manipulation; at frame-0 conditioning `real` visually tracks the GT arm descent (`strip_si0.jpg`).
- ❌ **Unreliable for ranking.** Rollout error accumulates; at mid-trajectory / higher-motion conditioning the gripper warps and arm pose diverges (`strip_si23.jpg`), inverting the ranking. Matches NVIDIA's "world models fall short as reliable simulators."

**Caveats bounding the verdict** (why "now", not "never"): free **Nano** (not Super 16B+), 480p / 30 steps, n=4, **WidowX** (not G1), and pixel-only metrics (no learned task-success reward). A production re-test would need **Cosmos3-Super on a rented GPU + a reward/success model**.

**Recommendation / integration point:** keep the MuJoCo geometric evaluator (`robot-agent/hardware/sim_evaluator/evaluate_policy.py`, consumed by `SimulationService` via the `backend: 'mujoco' | 'isaac'` union) as the **source of truth**. Do **not** add a `cosmos` backend now. If revisited, add it as a sibling **offline** `evaluate_wm.py` emitting the same `SimRunMetrics` schema — a *visual-OOD augmentation* stage, **never** the (diffusion-too-slow) RL inner loop. Reproduce: `cosmos3_wm_eval.py rollout --seqs 0,3,6,23 && cosmos3_wm_eval.py score` (needs HF PRO); metric self-test (no GPU): `cosmos3_wm_eval.py selftest`.

### 4.9 TASK-177 decision — G1 action-space mapping + Cosmos3-policy vs GR00T recommendation (2026-07-11)

**Verdict: DEFER Cosmos3-policy for G1 — the GR00T-N1.7 line is the humanoid path, and it is already proven in production on our stack.** This closes TASK-177 AC #1 (mapping decision) and AC #4 (recommendation) on paper; AC #2/#3 (fine-tune prototype + latency measurement) stay gated on a rented ≥H100 and are **not worth the spend now**.

**AC #1 — action-space mapping decision: custom 43-D joint-space head, NOT egocentric 57D.**
- Our G1 EDU embodiment is a fixed, well-defined joint space: **29 body DOF + 14 Dex3-1 hand DOF = 43-D**, exactly the `Unitree_G1_Dex3` LeRobot layout our whole pipeline already speaks (dataset conversion, `g1_dex3` modality config used for GR00T finetuning, telemetry, sim).
- Cosmos 3's **egocentric 57D** space is a human-egomotion representation (headset/hand-pose-centric) — mapping 43 robot joints through it adds a lossy retarget layer *inside the policy interface* for zero benefit when we own the robot's joint space end-to-end. It's the closest *documented* space, but the wrong abstraction.
- NVIDIA's own embodiment list shows a **custom high-DOF joint head is the supported route**: Agibot **29D** is a first-class fine-tune target, so the post-training scripts demonstrably accept non-standard, high-dimensional action heads. Decision: if a Cosmos3-policy G1 fine-tune is ever run, define a **custom 43-D head** (29 body + 14 hand, same ordering as `Unitree_G1_Dex3`), seeded from our existing LeRobot v3.0 G1 datasets.

**AC #4 — recommendation: stick with GR00T-N1.7 for the G1; do not pursue the Cosmos3-policy fine-tune now.**
1. **GR00T is already delivering on this exact robot, on our own hardware.** TASK-179/180 (2026-07-06…11, GPU_BOX): real GR00T-N1.7 **full finetunes** through the NeoDEM training UI on G1+Dex3 LeRobot data — incl. a 7-dataset multi-task merge (1,410 episodes, 7 language-conditioned tasks) whose 14k-step checkpoint beats the single-task baseline on **every** task. Training cost: $0 marginal (GPU box). A Cosmos3-Nano-Policy fine-tune needs a rented ≥H100 (16B BF16, many GPU-hours, §4.2–4.3) *before the first datapoint exists*.
2. **The Cosmos lineage argument favors GR00T too:** GR00T-N1.7's reasoning backbone *is* Cosmos-Reason. We already get NVIDIA's world-model pretraining benefits through the model line that has a **documented humanoid recipe**, first-class LeRobot support, and (per §4.8) none of the world-model-as-simulator reliability caveats in its deployment path.
3. **Closed-loop latency prior is poor:** 16B iterative-diffusion action generation vs GR00T-N1.7 (~3B-class) which already sustains our sim eval loop (15 Hz control with 8-step action chunks) on the GPU box. Measuring Cosmos3's real latency (AC #3) requires renting the GPU — do it only if reason #1 ever flips.
4. **No G1/bipedal embodiment exists in Cosmos 3's zoo** (§3) — we'd be the pathfinder, on rented silicon, against a baseline we already beat locally.

**Revisit triggers** (any one): NVIDIA ships a humanoid/G1(-like) Cosmos3-policy embodiment or checkpoint; GR00T line stalls on G1 task performance and dexterous-manipulation SOTA shifts to Cosmos3-policy; or a funded lab GPU budget makes the ~$50–150 spike (1×H100, custom-43D-head smoke run) free to us. The spike design if triggered: confirm `github.com/nvidia/cosmos` post-training accepts a custom 43-D embodiment config, run a ≤1k-step LoRA-style fit on one merged Pick* dataset, eval offline open-loop MSE vs the GR00T 14k checkpoint on held-out episodes — go/no-go before any full run.

### 4.10 TASK-182 outcome — DreamGen-style seed-to-scale on the GPU box; scale-out GO (2026-07-14)

**Verdict: GO for scaling neural-trajectory synthetic data — the full DreamGen recipe runs
locally on GPU_BOX (no rented GPU needed at 2B scale) and measurably improves the policy on
behaviors with zero real demonstrations.** This supersedes the TASK-175 forward-dynamics
pipeline for data *generation* (kept for visual augmentation); the §4.8 NO-GO on
world-model-as-simulator is unaffected (that's the evaluation use case).

**What was proven (details: `$UNITREE_ROOT/_data/task182_spike/LEARNING_REPORT.md`):**
1. **Stage 1–3 all-local:** Cosmos-Predict2-2B LoRA post-train on 202 real
   `G1_Dex3_PickBottle` episodes (~5 h, 23.4 GB) → 50 language-prompted neural videos
   (~2.5–4.7 min each) → GR00T-dreams IDM (trained from scratch on 182 real episodes)
   pseudo-labels with **holdout MAE 0.079 rad / 5.5 % of joint range = 4.2× better than the
   train-mean baseline** (AC 2).
2. **AC 3:** 50 episodes exported as valid **LeRobot v3.0 `Unitree_G1_Dex3`**, `_synthetic`
   + per-episode provenance JSON (EU-AI-Act-ready), registered and Playwright-verified in
   the RMS datasets UI; `neural_traj` WSL backend wired to the real pipeline.
3. **AC 4 (offline, N1-2B):** real-only vs real+45-dreams finetunes (identical
   hyperparameters): real holdout 6.4 % vs 6.5 % (no tax on the seen task); **held-out
   unseen-behavior dreams 16.8 % vs 7.7 % normalized MAE — 2.2× better** on cup-manipulation
   that has zero real episodes.
   ⚠ **Two corrections (TASK-185, data-checked against the parquet `task_index`):** (a) the 5
   held-out dreams are **pick** tasks (3× "Pick up the red cup", + place-left/place-right) —
   **not** the "push/slide/tip/knock" this section previously claimed; tip/knock appear in the
   *training* set (task_index 29, 33), never in the eval set. (b) Those three eval instructions
   also occur in the dreams arm's training set (different episodes), so this measures held-out
   *episodes of a seen instruction*, not an unseen instruction. The effect is real (real-only has
   zero cup episodes) but it is **not** unseen-verb generalization.

**Marginal economics:** after one-time post-train (+ one-time IDM per embodiment), an
additional dreamed episode costs ~2.5–5 GPU-minutes on the GPU box — no cloud spend. 14B/Cosmos3
on rented GPUs (§4.2–4.3) stays the *quality* upgrade path, to be triggered only if
closed-loop results plateau at 2B.

**Open before production scale-out (conditions on the GO):**
- **Closed-loop confirmation → RAN (TASK-185, 2026-07-17): it did NOT confirm the offline
  result. See §4.11.** The GO's economics and the "dreams teach behaviors with zero real
  episodes" claim are untouched, but the *evidence base* for it is now known to be weaker than
  §4.10 implies: the offline open-loop metric it rests on does not predict closed-loop behavior.
- **Dream-quality filter:** 2B dreams contain physics artifacts; add automated filtering
  (curation heuristics; optionally a TASK-176-style consistency scorer as a *filter*, which
  is a weaker requirement than the §4.8 simulator NO-GO) before large batches.
- Dream episodes train with zero state inputs (GR00T-dreams convention) — acceptable per
  upstream recipe, revisit if closed-loop results disappoint.

### 4.11 TASK-185 outcome — closed-loop Isaac eval on GR00T-N1.7: the offline result did NOT replicate (2026-07-17)

**Verdict: the §4.10 GO is NOT withdrawn, but its "closed-loop confirmation" condition is now
answered "no" for the seen task and "unanswerable in this sim" for the unseen behavior.**
Full report + raw JSON/mp4: `$UNITREE_ROOT/_data/task185/RESULTS.md`.

Setup: GR00T-N1.7-3B (not N1-2B), `NEW_EMBODIMENT`, both arms identical (3000 steps, batch 8,
single camera `cam_right_high`); live `Isaac-PickPlace-Cylinder-G129-Dex3-Joint` on DDS domain 1;
success read from the sim's own `rt/rewards_state`; 10 reset-isolated rollouts per cell.

| | offline real holdout | offline held-out dreams | **closed-loop place success** |
|---|---|---|---|
| real-only | 0.3811 rad / 25.6 % | 0.4147 rad / 27.4 % | **2/10** |
| real + dreams45 | **0.2363 rad / 16.0 %** | **0.2536 rad / 16.8 %** | **0/10** |

1. **The metrics contradict each other.** Offline, real+dreams wins 1.6× on *both* sets (a
   stronger result than §4.10's). Closed-loop, it completes the task less often than real-only
   (0/10 vs 2/10; Fisher exact p = 0.47 → not significant, i.e. **no measurable advantage**).
2. **Why: the offline metric rewards inaction.** A trivial "hold current state" policy scores
   **0.081 rad**, 3–5× better than either trained policy. The dreams arm is the more inert one
   (mean object displacement 0.265 m vs 0.409 m) — it wins offline *because* it moves less and
   loses closed-loop for the same reason. This is the §4.8 "do-nothing policy" pathology again,
   now demonstrated on the *action* metric that §4.10's GO rests on, not just on SSIM.
3. **The unseen-behavior half is un-measurable here.** `base_reward_pickplace_cylindercfg.py`
   only scores pick-and-place; the sim publishes no push/slide success. A derived proxy (lateral
   travel without lift) is invalid: *place*-instructed rollouts satisfy it **more** often (7–8/10)
   than *push*-instructed ones (6/10). **Blocker for any future attempt: add a push/slide reward
   to `unitree_sim_isaaclab/tasks/common_rewards/`.**
4. **Caveat — both arms are undertrained:** 3000 × batch 8 = 24k samples ≈ **0.15 epochs** of the
   157k-frame set. TASK-180's proven 14k-step N1.7 recipe suggests this buys only ~5 % (open-loop
   MSE 0.4036 @14k vs 0.4258 @2k), so a retrain is unlikely to flip the conclusion — but the
   comparison is currently between two weak policies.

**Net effect on the GO:** keep it — the marginal economics (~2.5–5 GPU-min/dreamed episode) and
the qualitative claim are unaffected, and real-only did complete the real task 2/10 with no
reward shaping. But **stop citing the offline 2.2× as evidence of policy improvement**: on this
data, open-loop MAE is anti-correlated with closed-loop success. Always report the hold-state
baseline beside it.

**Does this trigger the §4.10 "14B/Cosmos 3 quality upgrade path"? NO — not yet.** That path is
conditioned on "closed-loop results plateau at 2B"; we have not plateaued, we have failed to
*measure*. Dream quality is provably not the binding constraint: the **real-only** arm — trained on
182 real episodes and zero dreams — is equally bad (0.381 rad, 2/10). Better dreams cannot explain
or fix that, and generating them would still leave us unable to tell the arms apart. The binding
constraints are, in order: no push reward ([[TASK-186]]), a base policy stuck at 0–2/10 →
floor effect ([[TASK-188]]), and n=10 → p=0.47 ([[TASK-189]]); only then re-run the ablation
([[TASK-187]]). Those cost GPU-hours we already own. Cosmos 3 additionally costs cash (rented
GPUs — §4.2–4.3), is **preview-gated** for hosted access (§4.1), and its G1 action-space mapping
is still unproven ([[TASK-177]]) — so if the Cosmos 3 hypothesis is to be tested early, TASK-177
("can it drive a G1 at all?") is the cheap first move, not generation at scale.
